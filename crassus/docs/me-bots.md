Steve’s five inactive accounts use Crassus reconciliation, limit-order execution and the existing QQQ snapshot and quote route. Brian owns after-close integration. October–November informed the rules and is exploratory.

| Candidate | Entry on completed candles | Exit after entry |
|---|---|---|
| Golden-Time | Accelerating 9-minute close through prior range | 1 point behind best after +1 |
| Trend-Day | Golden-Time plus first 30-minute close beyond a predeclared level | 2 points behind best after +2 |
| Opening-Drive | 09:30–10:15 ET, three expanding 1-minute ranges, premarket level rejection in that sequence | 1-point trail or adverse 1-minute close, one attempt per direction |

The initial stop is the far end of the trigger. Directional reflection applies the same rules to puts. Acceleration means range at least 1.5 times the previous 14 ranges, close in the outer quarter and three strictly increasing ranges. For this candidate, prior range spans those same 14 completed session-aligned bars, trend breakout uses the previous 14 bars, and volume baseline uses the same 14. These definitions and the rejection/vertical guards require research-owner review before drawing strategy conclusions. Three losing paper round trips close entries for the session. A retry, rejected order or underlying move alone does not count as a realized loss. The one-try opening guard conservatively consumes an attempt when an executable intent is prepared.
